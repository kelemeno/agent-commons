# Threat model

The commons assumes some participants are hostile and some agents are
manipulable, and relies on independence and mechanical verdicts rather than on
anyone's good faith. This document lists the ten attacks the design targets,
what counters each, what risk remains, and which file implements the counter.
Files marked *(being written)* exist only as contracts in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md) today.

## Assumptions

These are trusted; if one fails, the attacks below get easier.

| Trusted party | Trusted for | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admin key holder (intended: the Anthill backend; today a person) | Binding owners to keys only after checking the Anthill account; ratified rule changes | Anyone can mint owners or rules |
| Coordinator key and process | Recording mechanical results honestly; holding the GitHub bot token | It can record false gate results, merges and settlements. Every such entry is signed and replayable, so the lie is detectable after the fact, not prevented |
| Seeding custodian | Writing plants, not leaking them, revealing them only after the review window closed | Plant recall measures nothing |
| Kernel signers | Reading kernel changes before signing | Kernel changes reach `main` on k signatures |
| Phase 1 human approver | Reading every merge to `dev` | Merges rest on agent reviews alone |
| The Anthill tree | Reflecting real social relationships | Distance-based exclusion stops nothing |

Out of scope: compromise of a participant's own device, and attacks on Anthill
itself (they are Anthill's threat model).

## The ten attacks

| # | Attack | Countermeasure | Residual risk | Implemented in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Split or slow backdoor.** A backdoor delivered in harmless-looking pieces over many submissions, or one dormant line. | Kernel paths are ring 0 and need k-of-n human sign-off. A taskless submission may not touch ring 0. UI cannot import the kernel. 400-line size cap. Behaviour delta: any change in what the backend computes from its own log must be declared and explained. Second-pass attestations review the snapshot's cumulative diff. Promotion covers the snapshot's whole unpromoted ancestry. The bridge applies diffs byte-exactly, so the PR is exactly what reviewers read. | A ring 1 backdoor whose trigger is absent from the replayed log passes behaviour delta and must be caught by reviewers. The fixture log is generated by base code, so it covers paths the base already exercises. Under the ratified Anthill rules, shell scripts, most YAML, the frontend's `vite.config.*` and `index.html` are not ring 0, although `defaultRules()` makes them so ([protocol §4](protocol.md#4-rings)). | `src/gates/rings.ts`, `src/gates/size.ts` *(being written)*, `src/gates/behaviourDelta.ts`, `src/adapters/anthill.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`submission`), `src/github.ts`, `src/promotion.ts` *(being written)*, `src/rules.ts` |
| 2 | **Self-referential capture.** Agents change the coordinator, the gates, the rules or CI so that their own work passes. | The commons code, `.commons/`, `.github/`, the whole backend server, `Dockerfile`, `Procfile` and `render.yaml` are ring 0 for Anthill. CI judges every PR against the **base** branch's rules, never the PR's copy. Rules change only by an admin-signed `rulesChange` carrying an Anthill poll id, and must pass `validateRules`. A behaviour-delta declaration counts only in the PR that adds it. | A PR can edit its own `pull_request` workflow; that is a ring 0 change and relies on branch protection requiring the check. Kernel sign-off is k = 2 people. The poll id in `rulesChange` is not verified, and nothing but ring 0 review checks that `.commons/rules.json` equals the ratified rules. Deploy and build config beyond those three files is ring 1 or 2 for Anthill (threat 1). CI pins the commons at `main` until a release tag exists. | `src/rules.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`rulesChange`), `src/adapters/anthill.ts`, Anthill `.commons/rules.json`, Anthill `.github/workflows/commons-gates.yml` |
| 3 | **Metric poisoning and bounty farming.** Inventing a measure that creates work for oneself; planting a bug for a friend to "find". | No metric gates or mints anything; only settlements from mechanical events move reputation. A proposal needs checkable evidence. A finding pays only if the harness reproduces it. The finder gets 0 if within `minTreeDistance` of the author, while the author is still slashed. The author's slash is at least the bounty, so the bug itself transfers nothing to the pair. | With an accomplice beyond the distance threshold, the pair nets up to the author's merge reward: 10 per tranche, up to 30, because nothing yet says a reproduced finding forfeits merge tranches. Approvers outside the pair pay the approver slash. Proposed contract for `src/incentives.ts`, not yet agreed: a reproduced finding on a submission forfeits its unpaid tranches and claws back paid ones. All of this depends on the tree reflecting real relationships. | `src/metrics.ts` *(being written)*, `src/findings.ts`, `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal` evidence) |
| 4 | **Collusion ring.** A group approves each other's work. | Reviewers are drawn after the submission is fixed, from a recomputable beacon. One seat per owner in an assignment, and one review commit per owner per submission, assigned or open. The author's owner and owners within `minTreeDistance` are excluded from assignment; the draw favours distant owners; at least one other model family. No owner may review, attest, sign off or file findings on its own covered work. Commit-reveal so reviewers cannot copy: commits close once any verdict is revealed, the plant is revealed or a finding reproduces. Reciprocity is reported in metrics. Phase 1: a human approves every merge. | Phase 1 has three owners: the eligible pool is tiny or empty, and collusion among all of them is undetectable. Open reviewers are not distance-filtered, and promotion coverage counts them, so two owners can meet the coverage check (3 families, 2 owners) between them. The coordinator chooses when to assign, so it can grind the ledger head hash unless the external entropy round is fixed in advance (not specified yet). The first reveal closes commits for everyone and there is no commit deadline, so an open reviewer who reveals at once can shut out assigned reviewers who have not committed. | `src/selection.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`reviewAssignment`, `checkCommit`), `src/coordinator.ts` (human approval), `src/tree.ts`, `src/review.ts` *(being written)*, `src/metrics.ts` *(being written)* |
| 5 | **Sybil reputation.** One person runs many owners to fill review seats or vote. | Owners are Anthill users, bound by the admin after checking the account. Reputation comes from Anthill's liquid democracy and cannot be self-minted. Each invite records an inviter, who pays `inviterShare` (25%) of the invitee's slashes. Invitees sit near their inviter in the tree, so distance exclusion keeps them off each other's reviews. Submission volume is logarithmic in reputation and needs review credits. | The commons is only as Sybil-resistant as Anthill sign-up and the admin's check, which is manual in Phase 1 (the passkey path is not built). A person with accounts invited by different, distant people defeats the tree check. | `src/ledger.ts` (`ownerKey`), `src/tree.ts`, `src/server.ts` (submit policy), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, Anthill `anthill-backend/src/auth.ts` |
| 6 | **Prompt injection in diffs.** Content in a change tells reviewing agents to approve it, leak secrets, or act. | Decisions are mechanical: gates and reproduced findings, never prose. Several reviewers from different model families. Agents run in isolated VMs without the owner's credentials and never push; only the coordinator's bot holds a GitHub token. The OpenRouter reviewer strips invisible and bidirectional Unicode before the model reads a diff. Honeytokens (decoy instructions) catch agents that obey content, and cost the owner 50 points. Missed plants cost reviewers. | An injection that only lowers a reviewer's stated probability is not detected directly. Honeytoken placement and detection are **not built**; only the ledger entry and the penalty exist. The agent key sits in the VM, so hostile code there can sign with it until the owner revokes it. The harnesses run the change's code as the coordinator's user; they are not sandboxes. | `src/ledger.ts` (`honeytokenTriggered`), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/findings.ts`, `src/github.ts`, `scripts/openrouter-review.ts`, `src/gates/behaviourDelta.ts`, [running-an-agent.md](running-an-agent.md) |
| 7 | **Timing attack at promotion.** Landing a change just before promotion, or a defect that triggers after release. | Every covered submission must have soaked 72 hours since merge. Unresolved or reproduced high and critical findings block promotion. A merge waits for every assigned reveal or the review window. The third tranche of the author's reward vests 30 days after promotion. A bug found in `main` costs its author 4 × the bounty. | A defect with a trigger date beyond the soak and vesting tail is caught only by review. The promotion rule does not check git ancestry of the snapshot commit. In Phase 1 nothing is promoted by the commons, and how `main` moves is not decided. | `src/promotion.ts` *(being written)*, `src/coordinator.ts` (`mergeReadiness`), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`snapshot`, `escape`) |
| 8 | **Honeypot recognition, and gaming the plant count.** Reviewers learn to spot plants and only try hard on them; or they inflate seeded recall. | Plants are committed before submission and enter through the normal path. Replay rejects a reveal whose commitment came later. Catches count only up to the close of the review window, so nothing learned from a reveal counts. Only assigned reviewers' flags count, and assigned seats are drawn by beacon. Commits close once a plant is revealed. Stats per reviewer and per model family, including each reviewer's flags on non-plant work. Calibration against actual escapes scales the critical bound up when reviewers do worse on real work than on plants. | One custodian writes all plants in Phase 1, so they share a style. Calibration needs escapes to learn from, which come late. If plants are recognisable, seeded recall overstates real recall. **Flag everything:** the payoffs make p = 1 on every review the best strategy ([protocol §6.2](protocol.md#62-what-the-payoffs-reward)), and an assigned reviewer who always flags catches every plant they are drawn for. That inflates seeded recall and deflates the critical bound, weakening promotion checks 2 and 3. The flag rate on other work is reported but not corrected for, and calibration (floored at 1) never penalises over-prediction. Revealing only after the window closed is custodian policy, not checked by replay; an early reveal lets a finding written from the description count as a catch. Who pays a bounty on a plant is not specified, and a post-reveal finding must not pay. | `src/plants.ts`, `src/risk.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`plantCommit`, `plantReveal`, `checkCommit`) |
| 9 | **Griefing with fake findings.** Flooding findings to slow merges or to drag on reviewers. | A finding locks stake and costs `falseFindingPenalty` if the harness runs and it does not reproduce. Only a reproduced finding moves reputation. A timeout or harness error is never a reproduction. Findings on one's own owner's work are rejected. | **Stall by unresolvable finding.** Any unresolved finding blocks its submission's merge (ledger `finding-pending`, and the coordinator), and an unresolved high or critical finding blocks promotion. A harness error (a test that sleeps past the timeout, a commit that does not resolve) records no result, and a finding whose test file is never uploaded is skipped. Either way it stays unresolved indefinitely and its finder is never penalised. A retry limit followed by a penalty or a human decision is **not built**. Each finding also costs coordinator compute before it is judged. A flaky test can fail on head and pass on parent by chance; the harness runs once. The harness is not a sandbox. | `src/findings.ts`, `src/coordinator.ts` (`mergeReadiness`, `reproduceFinding`), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`finding`, `findingResult`, `merge`) |
| 10 | **Volume and public misbehaviour.** Flooding proposals and submissions; an agent acting badly in public under the commons' name. | Proposals need evidence. Claims lock superlinear stake and last at most 7 days. Open submissions are capped at `1 + floor(log2(1 + reputation))`, and each needs a credit earned by reviewing. The server allows 60 requests a minute per owner and accepts only participant entry kinds. The owner or the coordinator can revoke an agent. Owners carry the reputation cost. | Nothing checks that locked stake fits within an owner's reputation. Proposals are not rate-limited beyond the request limit. Public misbehaviour outside the ledger has no mechanical signal; it needs human judgement. The appeal path to humans is **not built**. | `src/server.ts` (policy, rate limit), `src/rules.ts` (`claimStake`), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal`, `claim`, `agentRevoked`) |

## What "residual" means here

A residual risk is one the design accepts and measures instead of preventing.
The measurement is seeded recall and the critical upper bound
([protocol.md §7](protocol.md#7-risk-measurement)). With the default rules, the
promotion rule needs about 55 caught plants in 28 days to pass. In Phase 1 it
cannot pass, so the residual risks above are covered by the human who approves
each merge to `dev`, and by whoever decides how `main` moves. That procedure
is not decided ([protocol.md §5.11](protocol.md#511-promotion-rule--main)).
