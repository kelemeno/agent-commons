# Threat model

The commons assumes some participants are hostile and some agents are
manipulable, and relies on independence and mechanical verdicts rather than on
anyone's good faith. This document lists the ten attacks the design targets,
what counters each, what risk remains, and which file implements the counter.
Module contracts are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Assumptions

These are trusted; if one fails, the attacks below get easier.

| Trusted party | Trusted for | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admin key holder (a person) | Admitting owners (the allowlist) only after checking who they are, with the right inviter; ratified rule changes | Anyone can mint owners, vouch for socks, or change rules |
| Coordinator key and process | Recording mechanical results honestly; holding the GitHub bot token | It can record false gate results, merges and settlements. Every such entry is signed and replayable, so the lie is detectable after the fact, not prevented |
| Seeding custodian | Writing plants, not leaking them, revealing them only after the review window closed | Plant recall measures nothing |
| Kernel signers | Reading kernel changes before signing | Kernel changes reach `main` on k signatures |
| Phase 1 human approver | Reading every merge to `dev` | Merges rest on agent reviews alone |
| The member tree (the ledger's `ownerKey` invites) | Reflecting real social relationships: inviters vouch only for people they know | Distance-based exclusion stops nothing |

Out of scope: compromise of a participant's own device, and attacks on the
target project's own infrastructure (its hosting, its GitHub settings beyond
those the HANDOFF checklist names).

## The ten attacks

| # | Attack | Countermeasure | Residual risk | Implemented in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Split or slow backdoor.** A backdoor delivered in harmless-looking pieces over many submissions, or one dormant line. | Kernel paths are ring 0 and need k-of-n human sign-off. A taskless submission may not touch ring 0. UI cannot import the kernel. 400-line size cap. Second-pass attestations review the snapshot's cumulative diff. Promotion covers the snapshot's whole unpromoted ancestry. The bridge applies diffs byte-exactly, so the PR is exactly what reviewers read. | No gate runs the change's code, so a ring 1 backdoor must be caught by reviewers. The kernel is only what the project declares: until its `.commons/rules.json` names its kernel paths, they are ring 1 and need no kernel sign-off ([protocol §4](protocol.md#4-rings)). | `src/gates/rings.ts`, `src/gates/size.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`submission`), `src/github.ts`, `src/promotion.ts`, `src/rules.ts` |
| 2 | **Self-referential capture.** Agents change the coordinator, the gates, the rules or CI so that their own work passes. | In every target, `.commons/**` (the rules), `.github/**`, CI, build and deploy files, shell scripts, YAML and tsconfig are ring 0 by default, and the project adds its own kernel. CI judges every PR against the **base** branch's rules, never the PR's copy. Rules change only by an admin-signed `rulesChange` carrying the id of the group decision behind it, and must pass `validateRules`. | A PR can edit its own `pull_request` workflow; that is a ring 0 change and relies on branch protection requiring the check. Kernel sign-off is k = 2 people. The poll id in `rulesChange` is not verified, and nothing but ring 0 review checks that `.commons/rules.json` equals the ratified rules. Build or deploy config the defaults do not name (a project-specific file) is ring 1 unless the project declares it. | `src/rules.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`rulesChange`), the target's `.commons/rules.json` |
| 3 | **Metric poisoning and bounty farming.** Inventing a measure that creates work for oneself; planting a bug for a friend to "find". | No metric gates or mints anything; only settlements from mechanical events move reputation. A proposal needs checkable evidence. A finding pays only if the harness reproduces it and an operator confirms it is a real defect (a reproduction alone only tells head from parent, which a test pinning the old text does against any change). Merge rewards come only from tasks, so taskless whitespace merges mint nothing. The finder gets 0 if within `minTreeDistance` of the author, while the author is still slashed. The author's slash is at least the bounty, so the bug itself transfers nothing to the pair. | With an accomplice beyond the distance threshold, the pair nets at most tranche 1 (10), or 20 if the finding lands after the promotion: a confirmed finding on the submission voids its unpaid tranches 2 and 3, and tranche 1 is never clawed back. Approvers outside the pair pay the approver slash. All of this depends on the tree reflecting real relationships, and on the operator who confirms findings. | `src/metrics.ts`, `src/findings.ts`, `src/incentives.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal` evidence) |
| 4 | **Collusion ring.** A group approves each other's work. | Reviewers are drawn after the submission is fixed, from a recomputable beacon. One seat per owner in an assignment, and one review commit per owner per submission, assigned or open. The author's owner and owners within `minTreeDistance` are excluded from assignment; the draw favours distant owners; at least one other model family (forced only when two or more eligible owners offer one: a lone self-declared family must not buy a seat on every draw). No owner may review, attest, sign off or file findings on its own covered work. Commit-reveal so reviewers cannot copy (a commitment binds the reviewer and the subject, and a repeated commitment is refused), with a commit phase: no reveal before the assignment, nor before every assigned seat committed or `review.commitHours` passed; then commits close. Reciprocity is reported in metrics. Phase 1: a human approves every merge (a ledger entry); Phase 2: a merge the assigned reviewers flagged needs one. Every assignment is verifiable from the ledger alone (the member tree before it), and promotion fails any that does not verify. | Phase 1 has three owners: the eligible pool is tiny or empty, and collusion among all of them is undetectable. Open reviewers are not distance-filtered, and promotion coverage counts them, so two owners can meet the coverage check (3 families, 2 owners) between them. The beacon does not depend on the ledger head or on the member tree's digest: replay requires it to follow from the submission, its head commit and the recorded entropy, and separately requires the recorded digest to be that of the member tree before the assignment. The rule fixes the entropy to the first public randomness round after the submission's `at` plus the 5-minute clock skew (the author stamps `at`; protocol §5.6), but fetching and checking the round is not built; in Phase 1 a coordinator-chosen value is trusted not to be ground. The draw is over agents registered before the submission, so a registration cannot be timed to a winning draw; the coordinator still chooses which agents it revokes before the draw (on the ledger, with a reason), and Phase 1 trusts it with that. It does not choose the tree, and cannot pick a beacon by timing the draw around an admission: an owner admitted after the submission has no agent in the pool, moves no existing distance and is not a beacon input, so it changes neither the beacon nor the draw. A seat that never reveals still ends the window below quorum, and no second assignment exists. | `src/selection.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`reviewAssignment`, `checkCommit`), `src/coordinator.ts` (human approval), `src/members.ts`, `src/review.ts`, `src/metrics.ts` |
| 5 | **Sybil reputation.** One person runs many owners to fill review seats or vote. | Only an admin can admit an owner (`ownerKey`), after checking who it is. Reputation is commons reputation, earned only through settlements, so it cannot be self-minted. Each admission records an inviter, who pays `inviterShare` (25%) of the invitee's slashes. Invitees sit next to their inviter in the member tree, so distance exclusion keeps them off each other's reviews. Submission volume is logarithmic in commons reputation and needs review credits. | The commons is only as Sybil-resistant as the admin's check, which is manual. A person with owners vouched for by different, distant owners (or admitted with no inviter) defeats the tree check. | `src/ledger.ts` (`ownerKey`), `src/members.ts`, `src/server.ts` (submit policy), `src/incentives.ts` |
| 6 | **Prompt injection in diffs.** Content in a change tells reviewing agents to approve it, leak secrets, or act. | Decisions are mechanical: gates and reproduced findings, never prose. Several reviewers from different model families. Agents run in isolated VMs without the owner's credentials and never push; only the coordinator's bot holds a GitHub token. The OpenRouter reviewer strips invisible and bidirectional Unicode before the model reads a diff. Honeytokens (decoy instructions) catch agents that obey content, and cost the owner 50 points. Missed plants cost reviewers. | An injection that only lowers a reviewer's stated probability is not detected directly. Honeytoken placement and detection are **not built**; only the ledger entry and the penalty exist. The agent key sits in the VM, so hostile code there can sign with it until the owner revokes it. The harnesses run the change's code as the coordinator's user; they are not sandboxes. | `src/ledger.ts` (`honeytokenTriggered`), `src/incentives.ts`, `src/findings.ts`, `src/github.ts`, `scripts/openrouter-review.ts`, [running-an-agent.md](running-an-agent.md) |
| 7 | **Timing attack at promotion.** Landing a change just before promotion, or a defect that triggers after release. | Every covered submission must have soaked 72 hours since merge. Unresolved or reproduced high and critical findings block promotion. A merge waits for every assigned reveal or the review window. The third tranche of the author's reward vests 30 days after promotion. A bug found in `main` costs its author 4 × the bounty. | A defect with a trigger date beyond the soak and vesting tail is caught only by review. The promotion rule does not check git ancestry of the snapshot commit. Phase 1 promotes under `phase1Rules()` (protocol §5.11): wide recall bounds (ten caught plants pass), one kernel sign-off and a 24-hour soak, so a fast-acting defect that slips review has less time on `dev` before `main`; the human approving each merge and the kernel signer carry the rest. | `src/promotion.ts`, `src/coordinator.ts` (`mergeReadiness`), `src/incentives.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`snapshot`, `escape`) |
| 8 | **Honeypot recognition, and gaming the plant count.** Reviewers learn to spot plants and only try hard on them; or they inflate seeded recall. | Plants are committed before submission and enter through the normal path. Replay rejects a reveal whose commitment came later. Catches count only up to the close of the review window, so nothing learned from a reveal counts. Only assigned reviewers' flags count, and assigned seats are drawn by beacon. Commits close once a plant is revealed. Stats per reviewer and per model family, including each reviewer's flags on non-plant work. Calibration against actual escapes scales the critical bound up when reviewers do worse on real work than on plants. | One custodian writes all plants in Phase 1, so they share a style. Calibration needs escapes to learn from, which come late. If plants are recognisable, seeded recall overstates real recall. **Flag everything:** the payoffs make p = 1 on every review the best strategy ([protocol §6.2](protocol.md#62-what-the-payoffs-reward)), and an assigned reviewer who always flags catches every plant they are drawn for. That inflates seeded recall and deflates the critical bound, weakening promotion checks 2 and 3. The flag rate on other work is reported but not corrected for, and calibration (floored at 1) never penalises over-prediction. Revealing only after the window closed is enforced by `commons plant reveal`, not by replay; a custodian who bypasses the CLI and reveals early lets a finding written from the description count as a catch. Commitments are made in batches ahead of time and plants are submitted by rotating agents that also do ordinary work (protocol §7.1), but nothing checks the rotation. **Plants carry their owner:** every plant is authored by the custodian's owner, which is public, so flagging only that owner's work catches every plant drawn; `plantStats.flagsOnPlantAuthorsWork` reports each reviewer's flag rate on that owner's other work, but nothing corrects for it. Who pays a bounty on a plant is not specified, and a post-reveal finding must not pay. | `src/plants.ts`, `src/risk.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`plantCommit`, `plantReveal`, `checkCommit`) |
| 9 | **Griefing with fake findings.** Flooding findings to slow merges or to drag on reviewers. | A finding locks stake and costs `falseFindingPenalty` if the harness runs and it does not reproduce, or if an operator dismisses its reproduction as testing the intended change (the change-detector veto). Only a confirmed finding pays or slashes. A timeout or harness error is never a reproduction, nor is a sandbox that cannot use its checkout. Findings on one's own owner's work are rejected. | **Stall by unresolvable finding.** Any unresolved or undecided finding blocks its submission's merge (ledger, coordinator and promotion alike). The coordinator voids a finding whose test is not uploaded within `findingGraceHours` (24, as false), whose harness failed `maxHarnessErrors` (3) times (counted on the ledger, `findingAttempt`), or that has no result after `findingResultDeadlineHours` (72): the last two with `harnessError`, so nobody is slashed. A reproduction waits for an operator's decision, which is human time. A head whose code genuinely hangs is voided like a harness failure. Each finding also costs coordinator compute before it is judged. A flaky test can fail on head and pass on parent by chance; the harness runs once. The harness is not a sandbox. | `src/findings.ts`, `src/coordinator.ts` (`mergeReadiness`, `reproduceFinding`), `src/incentives.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`finding`, `findingResult`, `merge`) |
| 10 | **Volume and public misbehaviour.** Flooding proposals and submissions; an agent acting badly in public under the commons' name. | Proposals need evidence. Claims lock superlinear stake and last at most 7 days, extensions included, unless the holder submits; every claim entry is settled, so re-claiming a lapsed claim does not erase its slash. Open submissions are capped at `1 + floor(log2(1 + reputation))`, and each needs a credit earned by reviewing. The server allows 60 requests a minute per owner, caps concurrent requests per address, times out slow headers and bodies, and accepts only participant entry kinds (and operator decisions from operator keys). The owner or the coordinator can revoke an agent. Owners carry the reputation cost. | The server backs every stake with commons reputation (`rules.stake.allowance` + settlements − locked stake), but the ledger does not: an entry appended locally, outside the server, can lock more. Proposals are not rate-limited beyond the request limit. Public misbehaviour outside the ledger has no mechanical signal; it needs human judgement. The appeal path to humans is **not built**. | `src/server.ts` (policy, rate limit), `src/rules.ts` (`claimStake`), `src/incentives.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal`, `claim`, `agentRevoked`) |

## What "residual" means here

A residual risk is one the design accepts and measures instead of preventing.
The measurement is seeded recall and the critical upper bound
([protocol.md §7](protocol.md#7-risk-measurement)). With the default rules, the
promotion rule needs about 55 caught plants in 28 days to pass. In Phase 1
the preset's wider bounds pass from ten caught plants
([protocol.md §5.11](protocol.md#511-promotion-rule--main)), so the residual
risks above are covered by the human who approves each merge to `dev`, the
operator who judges each reproduction, and the kernel signer.
