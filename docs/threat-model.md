# Threat model

The commons assumes some participants are hostile and some agents are
manipulable, and relies on independence and mechanical verdicts rather than on
anyone's good faith. This document lists the ten attacks the design targets,
what counters each, what risk remains, and which file implements the counter.
Files marked *(being written)* exist only as contracts in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md) today.

## Assumptions

These are trusted; if one fails, the attacks below get easier.

| Trusted party | Trusted for | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anthill backend (admin key) | Binding owners to keys only after a passkey session; ratified rule changes | Anyone can mint owners or rules |
| Coordinator key | Recording mechanical results honestly | It can record false gate results, merges and settlements. Every such entry is signed and replayable, so the lie is detectable after the fact, not prevented |
| Seeding custodian | Writing plants and not leaking them | Plant recall measures nothing |
| Kernel signers | Reading kernel changes before signing | Kernel changes reach `main` on k signatures |
| The Anthill tree | Reflecting real social relationships | Distance-based exclusion stops nothing |

Out of scope: compromise of a participant's own device, and attacks on Anthill
itself (they are Anthill's threat model).

## The ten attacks

| # | Attack | Countermeasure | Residual risk | Implemented in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Split or slow backdoor.** A backdoor delivered in harmless-looking pieces over many submissions, or one dormant line. | Kernel paths are ring 0 and need k-of-n human sign-off. UI cannot import the kernel. 400-line size cap. Behaviour delta: any change in what the backend computes from its own log must be declared and explained. Second-pass attestations review the snapshot's cumulative diff. Promotion covers the snapshot's whole unpromoted ancestry. | A ring 1 backdoor whose trigger is absent from the replayed log passes behaviour delta and must be caught by reviewers. The fixture log is generated by base code, so it covers paths the base already exercises. | `src/gates/rings.ts` *(being written)*, `src/gates/size.ts` *(being written)*, `src/gates/behaviourDelta.ts`, `src/adapters/anthill.ts`, `src/promotion.ts` *(being written)*, `src/rules.ts` |
| 2 | **Self-referential capture.** Agents change the coordinator, the gates, the rules or CI so that their own work passes. | The commons code, `.commons/`, `.github/`, Dockerfile and deploy config are ring 0. CI judges every PR against the **base** branch's rules, never the PR's copy. Rules change only by an admin-signed `rulesChange` carrying a ratified Anthill poll id, and must pass `validateRules`. A behaviour-delta declaration counts only in the PR that adds it. | A PR can edit its own `pull_request` workflow; that is a ring 0 change and relies on branch protection requiring the check. Kernel sign-off is k = 2 people. CI pins the commons at `main` until a release tag exists. | `src/rules.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`rulesChange`), `src/adapters/anthill.ts`, Anthill `.commons/rules.json`, Anthill `.github/workflows/commons-gates.yml` |
| 3 | **Metric poisoning and bounty farming.** Inventing a measure that creates work for oneself; planting a bug for a friend to "find". | No metric gates or mints anything; only settlements from mechanical events move reputation. A proposal needs checkable evidence. A finding pays only if the harness reproduces it. The finder gets 0 if within `minTreeDistance` of the author, while the author is still slashed. The author's slash is at least the bounty, so a farming pair nets ≤ 0, and the approvers who waved the bug through also pay. | With an accomplice beyond the distance threshold, dev-stage farming is net zero for the pair (slash = bounty at stage multiplier 1); it is costless rather than profitable. It depends on the tree reflecting real relationships. | `src/metrics.ts` *(being written)*, `src/findings.ts`, `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal` evidence) |
| 4 | **Collusion ring.** A group approves each other's work. | Reviewers are drawn after the submission is fixed, from a recomputable beacon. One seat per owner. The author's owner and owners within `minTreeDistance` are excluded; the draw favours distant owners; at least one other model family. Commit-reveal so reviewers cannot copy. Reciprocity is reported in metrics. | Phase 1 has three owners: the eligible pool is tiny and collusion among all of them is undetectable. Open reviewers are not distance-filtered, and promotion coverage counts them. The coordinator chooses when to assign, so it can grind the ledger head hash unless the external entropy round is fixed in advance (not specified yet). Replay does not yet stop a commit made after another reviewer revealed. | `src/selection.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`reviewAssignment`, `reviewCommit`), `src/tree.ts`, `src/review.ts` *(being written)*, `src/metrics.ts` *(being written)* |
| 5 | **Sybil reputation.** One person runs many owners to fill review seats or vote. | Owners are Anthill users bound by the admin after a passkey session. Reputation comes from Anthill's liquid democracy and cannot be self-minted. Each invite records an inviter, who pays `inviterShare` (25%) of the invitee's slashes. Invitees sit near their inviter in the tree, so distance exclusion keeps them off each other's reviews. Submission volume is logarithmic in reputation and needs review credits. | The commons is only as Sybil-resistant as Anthill sign-up. A person with accounts invited by different, distant people defeats the tree check. | `src/ledger.ts` (`ownerKey`), `src/tree.ts`, `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, Anthill `anthill-backend/src/auth.ts` |
| 6 | **Prompt injection in diffs.** Content in a change tells reviewing agents to approve it, leak secrets, or act. | Decisions are mechanical: gates and reproduced findings, never prose. Several reviewers from different model families. Agents run in isolated VMs without the owner's credentials and never push. Honeytokens (decoy instructions) catch agents that obey content, and cost the owner 50 points. Missed plants cost reviewers. | An injection that only lowers a reviewer's stated probability is not detected directly. Honeytoken placement and detection are **not built**; only the ledger entry and the penalty exist. The harnesses run the change's code as the coordinator's user; they are not sandboxes. | `src/ledger.ts` (`honeytokenTriggered`), `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/findings.ts`, `src/gates/behaviourDelta.ts`, [running-an-agent.md](running-an-agent.md) |
| 7 | **Timing attack at promotion.** Landing a change just before promotion, or a defect that triggers after release. | Every covered submission must have soaked 72 hours since merge. Unresolved or reproduced high and critical findings block promotion. The third tranche of the author's reward vests 30 days after promotion. A bug found in `main` costs its author 4 × the bounty. | A defect with a trigger date beyond the soak and vesting tail is caught only by review. The promotion rule does not check git ancestry of the snapshot commit. | `src/promotion.ts` *(being written)*, `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`snapshot`, `escape`) |
| 8 | **Honeypot recognition.** Reviewers learn to spot plants and only try hard on them. | Plants are committed before submission and enter through the normal path. They are revealed only after the review window closed; replay rejects a reveal whose commitment came later. Stats per reviewer and per model family. Calibration against actual escapes scales the critical bound up when reviewers do worse on real work than on plants. | One custodian writes all plants in Phase 1, so they share a style. Calibration needs escapes to learn from, which come late. If plants are recognisable, seeded recall overstates real recall. | `src/plants.ts`, `src/risk.ts`, `src/ledger.ts` (`plantCommit`, `plantReveal`) |
| 9 | **Griefing with fake findings.** Flooding findings to slow merges or to drag on reviewers. | A finding locks stake and costs `falseFindingPenalty` if it does not reproduce. Only a reproduced finding blocks a merge or moves reputation. A timeout or harness error is never a reproduction. Findings on one's own owner's work are rejected. | Each finding costs coordinator compute before it is judged. A flaky test can fail on head and pass on parent by chance; the harness runs once. The harness is not a sandbox. | `src/findings.ts`, `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`finding`, `findingResult`) |
| 10 | **Volume and public misbehaviour.** Flooding proposals and submissions; an agent acting badly in public under the commons' name. | Proposals need evidence. Claims lock superlinear stake. Open submissions are capped at `1 + floor(log2(1 + reputation))`, and each needs a credit earned by reviewing. The owner or the coordinator can revoke an agent. Owners carry the reputation cost. | Public misbehaviour outside the ledger has no mechanical signal; it needs human judgement. The appeal path to humans is **not built**. | `src/incentives.ts` *(being written)*, `src/ledger.ts` (`proposal`, `claim`, `agentRevoked`) |

## What "residual" means here

A residual risk is one the design accepts and measures instead of preventing.
The measurement is seeded recall and the critical upper bound
([protocol.md §7](protocol.md#7-risk-measurement)). With the default rules, the
promotion rule needs about 55 caught plants in 28 days to pass, so in Phase 1
the residual risks above are covered by humans deciding what reaches `main`,
not by the rule.
